# Enchanted Realms: One Ring — Puzzle Ideas

*50 puzzles for review. Organized loosely by type. Not all will fit; meant as a broad brainstorm.*

-----

## Invisibility & The Ring

1. **The Watched Door** — A guard stares at a door the player needs to pass. No combat solution exists. The only way through is invisibility. Simple introduction to ring utility — and its cost.
1. **The Ceremony** — A public ritual is underway in the town square. A key NPC participating cannot be spoken to until the crowd disperses, which takes many turns — unless the player slips through invisibly to reach them mid-ceremony.
1. **The Pressure Plate Pair** — Two pressure plates in separate rooms must be activated simultaneously. The player must stand on one, go invisible, slip past an enemy to reach the second, and activate it before a timer resets the first.
1. **The Sleeping Guardian** — A creature that detects sound but not sight guards a chest. Approaching it normally triggers a noise check. Invisibility bypasses sound detection entirely — but the ring ticks the whole approach.
1. **The Thief’s Dilemma** — An item is locked inside a glass case in a room full of merchants. Taking it visibly triggers a chase. Taking it invisibly is clean — but if corruption is high enough, a phantom visibility roll (Mechanic #3 from Vol. 1) might expose the player anyway.
1. **The Mirror Gauntlet** — A corridor with three mirrors in sequence. Passing any mirror while visible sends the player back to the start of the corridor. The ring must stay on for all three — burning three turns of corruption minimum.
1. **The Invisible Witness** — A crime is being committed in a room. Watching it invisibly to the end reveals the perpetrator and unlocks a quest. Being visible scares them off before the reveal.

## Perception & Hidden Objects

1. **The False Wall** — A room that feels like a dead end. A perception check reveals a seam in the wall. A second, harder check (or Enchanted Glasses) reveals the latch. Two-stage hidden door.
1. **The Buried Message** — A garden or courtyard with a patch of disturbed soil. A perception check notices it; digging reveals a note that recontextualizes a quest or NPC relationship.
1. **The Hollow Sound** — Tapping the walls of a dungeon room produces a hollow echo in one corner (flavor text only). Players who try to interact with that corner find a hidden compartment. Rewards curiosity over rolling.
1. **The Wrong Room** — A room is described as empty. It has no exits listed. But a perception check reveals a trapdoor in the ceiling — not the floor — requiring the player to think vertically.
1. **The Switched Labels** — A row of three levers labeled Left, Center, Right. A perception check (or Enchanted Glasses) reveals the labels have been swapped. Pulling in the “correct” order triggers a trap; pulling in the visual-label order solves it.
1. **The Invisible Ink Note** — A blank piece of paper found early in the game. Bringing it to a specific light source (a candle in Will’s tower, the volcano’s glow) reveals writing — a clue to a hidden room or chest combination.
1. **The Camouflaged Creature** — A room with a dangerous creature that perfectly blends into the wall. Without a perception check, the player walks into combat with no warning. Success lets them choose to sneak, fight prepared, or retreat.
1. **The Second Portrait** — A painting on a wall. Examined once, it’s decorative. Examined with Enchanted Glasses equipped, a face in the background is actually a map showing a hidden room’s location.

## Environmental & Physics

1. **The Counterweight Door** — A heavy door won’t open by pushing. A chain on the far wall connects to a counterweight above the door. Pulling the chain (strength check) holds it open just long enough to pass through.
1. **The Flood Chamber** — A room that fills with water when entered (rising one unit per turn). The player must find a drain plug or an exit before it fills. The drain is behind a perception check.
1. **The Torch Sequence** — Four unlit torches in a room. Lighting them in the correct order (hinted at by symbols elsewhere in the dungeon) opens a door. Wrong order resets. Correct order is discoverable without brute force.
1. **The Unstable Bridge** — A rope bridge crossing a chasm. Walking at normal speed has a chance of a plank breaking (strength check to catch yourself). Walking very slowly (one move per two turns) avoids the check entirely.
1. **The Pressure Sequence** — Three floor tiles that must be stepped on in order. The order is carved above a nearby doorway — but the carving is in a language only readable with a lore item found elsewhere.
1. **The Dry Fountain** — A fountain in a courtyard, bone dry. Filling it (requires a water vessel from elsewhere) triggers a mechanism that rotates a nearby statue, revealing a passage behind its base.
1. **The Freeze Puzzle** — In a cold area, a pool of water can be frozen by dropping a specific reagent into it. Frozen, it becomes a walkable surface connecting two platforms that were otherwise unreachable.
1. **The Echo Chamber** — A large room where sound bounces. Making a loud noise (striking a weapon on the wall, shouting) causes a distant mechanism to trigger — opening a door in an adjacent room the player can’t currently see into.
1. **The Rolling Boulder** — Classic trap: entering a room triggers a boulder. The player has a fixed number of turns to find the exit before the boulder arrives. The exit requires a perception check to spot quickly.
1. **The Sinking Floor** — A room where the floor slowly sinks as the player’s weight shifts (one heart per two turns standing still). Moving continuously is harmless. The puzzle requires the player to interact with something that takes several turns — forcing a resource tradeoff.

## Logic & Deduction

1. **The Three Bottles** — Three unmarked bottles on a shelf. One is a healing potion, one is poison, one is water. Clues elsewhere in the dungeon (color descriptions, a note) narrow the options without making it a pure guess.
1. **The Lying Statues** — Two statues in a room, each with an inscription. One always tells the truth; one always lies. They give contradictory directions to an exit. A classic puzzle, grounded here by the fact that Enchanted Glasses can identify which is which.
1. **The Four Symbols Door** — A door with four rotating symbols (each with three positions = 81 combinations). The solution is not random — it matches a pattern seen in four different rooms explored earlier in the dungeon.
1. **The Alibi Chain** — An NPC accuses another NPC of stealing from the inn. Three witnesses give conflicting accounts. The player gathers testimony, finds the physical inconsistency (perception check), and names the actual thief to receive a reward.
1. **The Riddle Gate** — A gate with an inscription that poses a riddle. The answer is a word the player must type. The riddle’s solution is inferable from the game world — it references a location, item, or NPC the player has encountered.
1. **The Cipher Scroll** — A scroll written in a simple substitution cipher. The key is hidden in the same room (a second perception check). Decoding the scroll reveals the location of a hidden treasure.
1. **The Number Room** — Four rooms, each containing a number written in a different format (tally marks, Roman numerals, spelled out, dots). They must be added and the sum entered on a combination lock. Tests careful reading, not math.
1. **The Weight of Guilt** — An old balance scale in a dungeon room. Placing items on each side in the correct combination (matching a total weight hinted at by a nearby inscription) unlocks a hidden drawer beneath it.

## NPC & Social Puzzles

1. **The Password Chain** — An NPC will only speak a password if the player first gives them an item. That item is held by a second NPC who wants something else. A three-way chain of trades, none of which are spelled out — only hinted at in dialogue.
1. **The Feuding Siblings** — Two NPCs in different parts of town won’t cooperate with the player because of a grudge. Resolving the grudge (finding and delivering a lost letter between them) unlocks both as quest givers and a joint reward.
1. **The Unreliable Witness** — An NPC gives the player directions to a location — but they’re wrong (perception check on their demeanor reveals uncertainty). Players who follow blindly get lost; players who investigate find the real path.
1. **The Impersonation** — A quest requires the player to convince an NPC they are someone else. Requires having the right item (a stolen seal, a uniform), saying the right things in dialogue, and passing a social roll. Failing locks out the quest path.
1. **The Mediation** — Two factions in the town have a dispute. The player must gather each side’s grievance, find the compromise solution hidden in the lore of the dispute, and present it to both simultaneously at the inn.
1. **The Reluctant DM** — One DM (Litlock, given his hidden nature) won’t share information unless the player makes them laugh. A set of absurd optional dialogue choices exists; only the right sequence earns his cooperation for a specific secret.
1. **The Corrupted Witness** — An NPC who saw something important has been frightened into silence. Wearing the ring near them makes them more afraid. Removing it and approaching slowly, combined with a social roll, earns their trust.

## Multi-Step & Multi-Room

1. **The Organ Pipe Room** — A room with five pipe openings in the wall, each producing a different tone when air passes through. A tune is written on a stone tablet in a previous room. Playing the tune in order opens a vault.
1. **The Ghost Trail** — Objects in a haunted wing of a dungeon have been moved from their original positions (described in an old inventory list found at the entrance). Returning them all to their correct spots lays the ghost and opens a sealed door.
1. **The Ritual Circle** — Five candles placed around a symbol on the floor. Lighting them in the wrong order extinguishes the previous ones. The correct order is encoded in a poem found in the dungeon — one line per candle.
1. **The Timed Relay** — A mechanism requires three levers in three rooms to be pulled within a short window. Impossible alone — except that pulling the first lever freezes an enemy that was blocking a shortcut, making the sequence just achievable solo if the route is known.
1. **The Sunken Key** — A key sits at the bottom of a deep well. It can’t be reached by hand. A series of items must be combined (a string, a hook, a weighted object) to fish it out — none of which are in the same room as the well.
1. **The Color Sequence** — A mosaic floor with colored tiles. Stepping on them in the wrong order resets the puzzle. The correct path is painted in miniature on the ceiling — visible only when lying down (a specific command or Enchanted Glasses).
1. **The Moving Room** — A room that slowly rotates, changing which exits connect to which adjacent rooms. The player must time their exits to reach the correct destination, using a pattern that can be learned by observation.
1. **The Shadow Puzzle** — A room with a single light source and several objects that cast shadows. Arranging the objects so their shadows form a specific shape on the wall (described in a clue) opens a compartment.

## Volcano & Endgame

1. **The Lava Step** — In the volcano area, stepping stones cross a lava channel. Each stone has a symbol. Only stones with symbols matching a pattern learned earlier in the area are safe — others sink. Wrong choices cost hearts, not instant death.
1. **The Ring’s Last Test** — The final approach to completing the main quest requires passing through a room where the ring actively resists removal (Mechanic #54 from Vol. 2 in full effect). The puzzle is not a lock or a lever — it is the player deciding whether to keep the ring on to finish faster, or remove it and take the slower, safer path. There is no wrong answer mechanically. There is only the choice, and the corruption counter.
